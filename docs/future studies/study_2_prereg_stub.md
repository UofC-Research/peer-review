# Study 2 — Contextual Heterogeneity in Peer-Review–Associated Manuscript Change

**Status:** Planned (Design stub only; not preregistered)  
**Dependency:** Study 1 completion and lock required

---

## Purpose

Study 2 will examine whether **within-manuscript changes in statistical rigor indicators** (V1–V10), as quantified in
Study 1, are **associated with contextual factors** related to authors and research environments.

Study 2 is explicitly secondary to Study 1 and treats Study 1 outputs (e.g., ΔVₖ, PRES) as fixed inputs.

---

## Conceptual Scope (High-Level)

Study 2 will explore descriptive associations between Study 1 change metrics and contextual factors such as:

- Funding environment (e.g., soft-funded vs hard-funded systems)
- Author career stage
- Institutional or regional academic system
- Scientific discipline

These factors are treated as **descriptive moderators**, not causal drivers.

---

## Design Constraints (Hard Boundaries)

- No Study 2 variables are recorded, inferred, or inspected during Study 1.
- Study 2 will use the **same sampling frame** as Study 1 (bioRxiv and medRxiv only).
- No Study 2 analyses will be conducted prior to Study 1 planning lock.
- Study 2 will be preregistered **separately** after Study 1 completion.

---

## Interpretation Framework

All findings in Study 2 will be interpreted as **associational and exploratory**.

Study 2 does not estimate causal effects of peer review or contextual factors and does not revise Study 1 indicators,
scoring rules, or composite measures.

---

## Deferred Decisions

The following elements are intentionally unspecified and will be defined only at full preregistration:

- Operational definitions of contextual variables
- Statistical models and estimands
- Power considerations
- Missingness handling and exclusion rules

---

## Relationship to Other Studies

- **Study 1:** Establishes baseline measurement and distributions
- **Study 2:** Examines contextual heterogeneity within the same sampling frame
- **Study 3:** Addresses external validity across preprint ecosystems
