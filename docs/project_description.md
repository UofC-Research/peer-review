# Publish or Perish: Quantifying the Impact of Peer Review on Scientific Work

## Background

Publish or perish is the norm in science, regardless of specific fields. Therefore, going through peer review is the process that everyone has to struggle with. However, the conventions of peer review in different disciplines may be quite different: some reviewers may require the authors to change the work dramatically while some only provide minor suggestions. Although sometimes the suggested changes may be constructive, in many cases the authors do not like them. When unpleasant comments are proposed, the authors’ willingness of following review comments to change their manuscripts are different, depending on their desperation to publish this work in the desired journal. In some fields, e.g., biomedical research, sometimes the scientific work has to be changed significantly after going-through peer-review, costing efforts not aligned to the authors’ original scientific motivations. As such, there are some questions on whether peer review indeed helps improve scientific rigor, or it actually could harm the academic freedom of authors.

## Aims

The motivation of this project is to quantify the changes of the research papers due to the process of peer review, and associate this quantified changes with various factors including: 
* The funding mechanism of the authors’ institutions – soft funded or hard funded. Here “soft” means the faculty members’ salary is largely covered by external grants, instead of the university/institute. 
* The internal competition within the systems that the authors have to survive: Europeans, Americans, and Asians. 
* The career stage of the corresponding authors and first-authors. A research track, tenure track, or tenured professor may treat the review comments differently. 
* The disciplines: Math, Stats, Computer, Physics, Chemistry, Biology, and Medicine. 

In this work, we will quantify the level of changes of an article before and after peer review based on the comparison between their preprint in BioRxiv, Arxiv, and MedRxiv, and their final publication in journals by: 
* conducting statistical association analysis to figure out the contributions of the above hypothetical factors to the changes due to peer reviews.

## Impact

This project has practical and intellectual implications:
- For authors: it provides insight into what kinds of changes are typically expected in different fields and journals,
  helping to guide manuscript preparation and submission strategy.
- For students: it offers data-driven guidance for those considering PhDs, especially in evaluating the pressures and
  review cultures of different disciplines.
- For policymakers and funders: it contributes to the debate on how peer review affects research quality and academic
  freedom.

Ultimately, this work seeks to move beyond anecdotal complaints and provide a quantitative foundation for reforming or rethinking the peer review process.
